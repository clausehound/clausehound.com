Clausehound · Legal knowledge engine

Legal knowledge, as data your agents can cite.

Clausehound turns statutes, regulations and contracts into structured, versioned data: every provision addressable, every amendment kept. Your apps and AI agents query it over GraphQL and MCP, and get back the exact clauses with their sources, not a 400-page PDF.

What it is

One model for every legal document.

A statute, a regulation and a supply agreement are the same thing to Clausehound: a document with versions, broken into a tree of clauses that can be tagged, compared and cited. Build on the model once, and it works for all of them.

{% comment %}The real model: Contract → ContractVersion → Cluster → Clause, with tag groups (clusterCategories) inherited down the tree. Public names are the ones in docs/LawyerOverview.md.{% endcomment %}
  1. Document Employment Standards Act, 2000 · S.O. 2000, c. 41
  2. Version As it read from 2009-11-06 · immutable
  3. Cluster Part VII › Hours of work › s. 17
  4. Clause 17 (1) The provision text, with its stable identity
  5. Tags Attached at any level and inherited down the tree

  1. Ingest

    Statutes from official consolidations, contracts from PDF and Word. Parsing and OCR turn them into the clause tree, headings and all.

  2. Version

    Every consolidation or draft becomes an append-only version. Nothing is overwritten, so any two dates can be compared.

  3. Tag

    A controlled taxonomy marks what each clause does. AI proposes tags; people confirm them, on the record.

  4. Serve

    GraphQL for your apps, MCP for your agents, and CSV, JSON or Excel for everyone else.

Architecture

Fast, small, and cheap to run.

The hard work happens once, at ingest. After that, a question is a database lookup over structured clauses, not a model reading a document from scratch. That's what keeps responses fast and token bills low.

Core

Rust, async, end to end.

The API is Rust on Actix and Tokio, with GraphQL queries batched through dataloaders and models generated by our own derive macros. Small memory footprint, predictable latency.

Deployment

Distributed by design.

Each client gets its own deployment and endpoint, like yourbusiness.clausehound.com. Services run as separate containers, so your data stays in your instance and each part scales on its own.

Tokens

Agents ask for clauses, not documents.

A tool call returns the provisions that answer the question, with section paths and sources. Your agent's context stays small, so calls are cheaper and faster than pasting whole Acts into a prompt.

Client

Diffs in the browser.

Version comparison runs as Rust compiled to WebAssembly, and app state is reduced in a shared worker, so the interface stays responsive on documents with thousands of clauses.

Security

Untrusted files stay boxed in.

PDF parsing and OCR run in their own service on an isolated internal network. Sign-in is JWT-based, with Azure AD and OpenID Connect for single sign-on.

Change control

Append-only history.

Versions are immutable and chained. When a new consolidation lands, anything that cited a changed clause is surfaced for review, automatically.

Verification

No citation, no claim.

Language models are fluent and sometimes wrong, and in law a confident wrong answer is the expensive kind. Clausehound puts guardrails between the model and the answer.

  • Grounded in the source. Every answer resolves to a specific clause, in a specific version, with its section path and source. The model doesn't get to paraphrase the law from memory.
  • Citations are checked. A section the model cites has to exist in the version in force on the date asked about. If it doesn't, the answer is flagged, not passed through.
  • AI proposes, people verify. Tags and findings carry a review state. Lawyers confirm or correct them in the app, and who verified what, and when, is kept.

The knowledge base

Not US-centric. Canadian law, done properly.

Most legal AI is trained and tested on American law, and treats Canada as an edge case. Clausehound's corpus is built from official Canadian consolidations, federal and provincial, with every historical version kept, so it answers the questions Canadian businesses actually face.

Integrations

Bring your own agent.

Clausehound speaks MCP, the open standard AI assistants use to connect to tools. Point Claude, or any MCP client, at your endpoint and it can search, compare and cite the law directly. Your apps get the same data over GraphQL.

{% comment %}Tool names are the shipped ones in rust/clausehound/src/mcp.rs (docs/retention/mcp/tool-catalog.md). The dialogs are simplified, not copies of either product's actual UI.{% endcomment %}
  • MCP server JSON-RPC over HTTP. Find statutes, list versions, search text, diff any two versions, fill schedules.
  • GraphQL API The same model your apps use, with subscriptions over WebSocket for live updates.
  • Exports CSV, JSON and Excel of any analysis, straight into the spreadsheet your team already has.
  • Documents in PDF, Word and official statute consolidations, parsed into the clause tree.
  • Single sign-on Azure AD and OpenID Connect, with JWT for service-to-service calls.
  • Point-in-time law Every version of every statute we track, comparable between any two dates.
The patent

A taxonomy for clauses, patented.

The heart of Clausehound is protected by Clause Taxonomy System and Method for Structured Document Construction and Analysis, US Pat. No. 12,670,200.

In plain terms: a way of breaking legal documents into typed, nested clauses, so they can be built, versioned, compared and analysed as data, rather than as text. It's why the same machinery handles a statute and a contract, and why a change to one clause can be traced everywhere it matters.
